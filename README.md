# Thesis Studio

A fast desktop workspace for writing an MBA thesis: read and annotate papers, collect quotes into notes, and work with an AI research assistant that runs on your **ChatGPT or Claude subscription**.

## Run it

```bash
bun install      # installs deps and copies pdf.js assets
bun run dev      # development with hot reload
bun run build && bun run start   # production build
bun run dist     # macOS .dmg in dist/
bun run test     # unit tests (statistics, scoring keys, dataset parsing, citation support, typed citations, APA 7 references, printed page numbers)
```

**Assistant requirement:** pick the subscription in **Settings → Assistant**. No API key is needed for either one.

- **ChatGPT:** the [Codex CLI](https://github.com/openai/codex) (`npm i -g @openai/codex`), signed in with ChatGPT (`codex login`, or **Connect ChatGPT** in the app). Models: GPT-6 Luna and GPT-6 Sol.
- **Claude:** the [Claude Code CLI](https://claude.com/claude-code) (`curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash`), signed in with your Pro/Max plan (`claude auth login`, or **Connect Claude** in the app). Models: Sonnet 5.5 (default) and Opus 5.5.

The chat's **effort** (Low → Max, plus Ultra on GPT-6 Sol) defaults to **High** (Claude Sonnet 5.5 is the default model) and can be changed in Settings or right in the composer. Writing helpers, translations and metadata extraction keep their own lighter effort.

Your library lives in `~/Documents/Thesis Studio/`. PDFs go in `Papers/`; notes, highlights, chats, tasks and reading lists are plain JSON in `.data/`, so they are easy to back up or sync.

## What it does

| Area | Features |
|---|---|
| **General assistant** | One conversation about your whole workspace (⌘⌥A, *Asistente* at the top of the sidebar, or the prompt box on Home). It sees a catalog of your library (abstracts, reading-guide summaries, matrix rows, status, tags, lists), your notes, tasks and thesis, plus what you were just looking at (the focus chip: «este paper» means the paper you had open, on the page you were on). Ask what to read next, for readings to dig into a topic, for papers related to the one you're reading, or how your week looks; recommendations come from your library first, each with a button to open it. It can also **do things**: add papers to reading lists, mark them as read or to read, star and tag them, create or reschedule tasks, write a new note or append to one, build a reading guide. Everything it proposes arrives as a checklist you review and apply with one click, and can undo. When it needs what a paper actually says, it asks to read the full text (or a note), the app hands it over and it continues, citing pages you can click. The paper and note chats on the right can act the same way («agregá este paper a las candidatas»). Conversations are saved as they happen, even an answer still arriving when you quit (it's kept, marked as cut off, with a way to ask again). **New conversation** keeps the current one in the **history** (the clock icon in the chat header), per context, so you can reopen or delete any past conversation; the assistant picks up its full transcript when you go back to one. |
| **Marco de investigación** | The course's research framework, always applied (⌘⌥M, or the second entry in the sidebar, which shows your pair and how many points need a look). The mandated theme (transformational leadership, attachment, the leader as security provider, empathy and OCB/CCO); pick exactly **2 of the 5 topics** (a 3rd is refused), each with its instrument, scoring, reverse items and columns in the course's Qualtrics dataset (ECR-RS, EQ-29 Chile, HSA-TFL, LSPS, CCO); every measure from the follower's point of view; demographics as extra crosses; your title, question and hypotheses. **Live checks** flag a title or question that misses one of your topics, brings in one outside the pair, or doesn't state the follower's perspective. **Aplicado en el asistente** proves it: it builds the real instructions of every assistant (general, paper, note, thesis, tasks, wiki, matrix, reading guides, writing helpers, autocomplete, recall grading, and the analysis results the agent sees) and checks each carries the framework, your pair, demographics, hypotheses, question, deadline, the UCEMA formal rules and the AI-use policy, with the exact text one click away. Translation and metadata extraction are listed as left out on purpose, with the reason. The same proof lives in **Settings → Reglas y verificación**, next to the live UCEMA requirements of your thesis (word limit, format, authorizations, unreviewed AI drafts, the deadline and the email to send the PDF to). Under the pair, **coverage** counts how many papers in your library cover each of your topics and how many cross both; fewer than 5 crossing papers raises a gap warning, and each count opens the matrix filtered. The calendar is the 3rd-term workshop's: it started on 24/9 and has a delivery every 4 weeks (15/10, 12/11 and 10/12, the last one being the final work, with the tutor's approval a week before). Each of the course's stages belongs to a delivery (1: question, hypotheses and method; 2: literature, theory, data and analysis; 3: results, discussion, introduction and conclusions, abstract and a full read); **Ajustar el plan** puts each stage in its delivery's weeks with pomodoro plans and milestones for each delivery and the tutor, moves plan tasks whose dates drifted and drops milestones the calendar no longer has, undoable in one click. Home shows the pair, the countdown to the next delivery and the current stage; Settings shows a summary. The course's data-analysis guide (α ≥ .70, skewness, ±3 SD outliers, Pearson/Spearman, Bonferroni/FDR, regression with VIF) runs in the **Análisis de datos** tab. The thesis checklist adds the course's writing advice: no period on level 1–3 headings, paragraphs over 25 lines, whether the body covers your 2 topics; the style review flags «el mismo» used as a pronoun, filler words like «realizar», stacked -mente adverbs and sentences over 30 words. |
| **Análisis de datos** | The course's Qualtrics dataset analysed in the app (⌘⌥Y, or *Análisis* in the sidebar), following the course's *Continuous Data Analysis Guide* step by step. Import the `.xlsx` or `.csv` export, numeric or labelled (both give the same numbers); several imports can live side by side and a selector in the header picks the one analysed. Only valid responses count: finished, not a survey preview or test, and with informed consent; responses faster than a third of the median duration are flagged and can be left out. **Participants**: who was left out and why, and an APA table of the sample (age, gender, education, position, experience, tenure, industry). Every scale is scored with its key: ECR-RS avoidance and anxiety per relationship and overall, EQ-29 scored 0-1-2 with its reverse items, HSA-TFL and its four subscales, LSPS, CCO-I and CCO-O; up to 20% missing items are prorated. Reverse items are editable if your questionnaire differs. Then: **reliability** (Cronbach's α with the guide's poor / moderate / good / excellent reading, flagged below .70), **descriptives** (n, M, SD, min, max, range, skewness read as mild / moderate / strong, kurtosis, ±3 SD outliers, optional winsorizing; histogram and normal Q-Q plot of any variable), **correlations** among your pair (Pearson or Spearman picked by normality, or forced; FDR or Bonferroni, uncorrected p in the table with † on those that survive; one-tailed for directional hypotheses), **attachment per relationship** against your other topic, the **comparison of two correlations** (Fisher's z, section 7 of the guide), **demographic crosses** (Welch t / Mann-Whitney, ANOVA / Kruskal-Wallis, Bonferroni, groups under 5 left out) and a **regression** of one topic on the other (you pick the outcome) plus your demographics, continuous ones standardized and two-group ones as 0/1 (standardized β, partial r, R², F, VIF, Bonferroni across regressions, residual normality with histogram and Q-Q, partial regression plot), with an optional **moderator** (ΔR² and the interaction terms). Each result has its APA 7 table and a grayscale figure, ready to copy or insert into the thesis. The assistants get the computed numbers (and are told never to invent statistics); *Interpretar con el asistente* asks for guidance on reading them, not for the text. |
| **AI-use policy** | The UCEMA rules (8.e) allow AI for correcting writing, grammar, format and APA, but not for replacing your analysis; Turnitin flags AI text. In **Settings → Reglas y verificación** you choose: no drafts, or **drafts with a warning**. With drafts on, the thesis assistant coaches first and only writes a draft when you ask, never for interpretation, discussion or conclusions; the draft comes as a card, inserting it takes an explicit acknowledgement, and the text stays underlined as an AI draft (and blocks the Requisitos checklist) until you rewrite it and mark it *Revisado*. Drafts, accepted writing-helper rewrites and autocomplete are logged; **Exportar registro** saves the log as Markdown for your tutor. Kept in `.data/ailog.json`. |
| **Progress** | Home and Tasks show where the thesis stands: words toward the 18,000 limit with a 30-day sparkline (a snapshot a day, `.data/progress.json`), focus time today and this week with the plan's stages, and a **burndown** of the plan's pomodoros against a straight line to the tutor's approval (a week before the deadline): *En ritmo* or *Atrasado*. The timeline draws the tutor and submission lines. The assistants get the same facts with each message, plus how many papers cover your pair. |
| **Reading lists** | Group papers in lists: **Candidatas para la tesis** is the shortlist for your research, and you can make others («Capítulo 2», «Para profundizar»). Right-click a paper in the library to add it or make a list; lists show as chips above the library to filter by (in the list's own order), candidates carry a pink bookmark, and the assistant can manage them for you. Kept in `.data/lists.json`. |
| **Library** | Drag PDFs anywhere. Duplicate detection. Automatic metadata: DOI → Crossref, title search → Crossref, then AI extraction as a fallback. Status (to read / reading / read), stars, tags and reading progress. |
| **Reader** | pdf.js with virtualized rendering. Text selection. 5 highlight colors, comments, find in paper, highlights translated into Spanish automatically (the Highlights panel and the highlight's popover show the Spanish first and the original below, like notes; the translation is stored on the highlight and reused when you send it to a note), pinch/⌘± zoom, night reading, and it resumes where you left off. |
| **Reading guide & skim mode** | One click maps a paper before you read it: a three-line TL;DR, how it bears on *your* thesis, a section roadmap marked **Read / Skim / Skip**, key terms, and questions to read with. **Skim mode** (⌘⌥S) underlines the key sentences right on the page by role: goal, method, finding, limitation. Step through them with J / K, or click one to highlight, note, explain or ask. When the paper defines its key terms, those defining sentences are found too: they get their own **Definition** marks in skim mode, sit under each term in the Guide tab with a jump link, and *Resaltar todas* turns them into purple highlights with the term as the comment. Shows full-read and skim time. Guides are cached in `.data/guides.json`; quotes are located in the PDF text, so marks land on the exact words even when the model gets the page wrong. |
| **Recall check & Repaso** | When you finish a paper (or from the Guide tab), answer its 3–4 core questions from memory while the PDF is blurred. The assistant grades each answer against the paper (Bien / Incompleta / Faltó, with what was missing and the pages to reread), your answers go to the paper's note under *En mis palabras*, and the paper comes back for review in 1 day if recall was weak, then 2, 5, 12… days. Due reviews, and finished papers never checked, show under **Repaso** on Home. Retrieval practice and spacing are the two study techniques with the strongest evidence (Dunlosky et al., 2013). Kept in `.data/recalls.json`. |
| **Term definitions & library citations** | Key terms from the reading guide get a dotted underline on the page; hover for the Spanish definition and the paper's own wording, click to jump to where the paper defines it. In-text citations of papers already in your library ("Popper & Mayseless, 2003") are tinted blue, or green once you've read them; hover to see which paper, click to open it. Toggle with the book button in the reader toolbar. |
| **Literature matrix** | Every paper in the same comparable columns: question, theory, method, context and sample, findings, limitations, and relevance to your thesis (⌘⌥L, or *Literature matrix* in the sidebar). Filled from the reading guides; *Completar guías* builds the missing ones one by one. Each paper is tagged with the **course topics** it studies (from its reading guide, else its title and abstract; edit them in the paper's info panel), shown as chips; filter by topic or by **Cruce** (papers on both your topics). Search, filter by relevance, sort by relevance, year or author, click a row to expand it, and export to CSV or copy as a Markdown table. |
| **Selection toolbar** | Highlight (`H`), send quote to note (`N`), explain (`E`), ask (`A`), copy. |
| **Thesis** | UCEMA MBA template built in: cover, dedication, acknowledgments, abstract + keywords, live table of contents, body, APA 7 (Spanish) references generated from your citations (inserted with Citar or typed by hand as «(Autor, año)» or «Autor (año)», matched to your library by surname and year, «citado en» counting only the source you read; copies of a paper in the library make one reference, the most complete record), appendices and the authorizations page. Every section is a Letter sheet in Calibri 11, double spaced, 1.27 cm indents and 2.54 cm margins. Five APA heading levels (4–5 run in, type `####`), footnotes (⌘⌥F), the template's guidance as an ⓘ next to each heading and sheet label (hover to read it, never in the way of the text), live word count against the 18,000-word limit and a Requisitos checklist (among its citation checks: every citation in the text has its reference, same author and year needs 2019a/2019b, incomplete references, titles not in sentence case, and two citation parentheses in a row). **APA tables and figures** inserted from the analysis are numbered automatically, editable in place (title, note, cells), and the checklist asks that each one be mentioned in the text; only their title and note count toward the word limit. Exports to Word (native footnotes, TOC field, page numbers, APA tables, figures as images) and PDF (paginated with Paged.js, Calibri embedded from your local Office install, Carlito otherwise). Writing help: a floating toolbar on any selection (synonyms, corrections, rewrites, connectors, title case), a style review that underlines calques and filler, ghost-text autocomplete that finishes the sentence you started, and «Ideas» (guiding questions, no text written for you). The writing helpers and autocomplete also get the course framework. |
| **Citation fixes by the assistant** | In the thesis chat, ask it to check the citations (*Verificar APA*, or *Corregir citas de un párrafo* and paste the paragraph): it reads the thesis with its citations as written, explains each error (one parenthesis for several works in alphabetical order, «y», et al., missing year or page) and proposes the fixes as changes you apply with one click and can undo. Only the citation changes, never your words: a fix that would touch anything else, or isn't a citation, doesn't apply. Works of your library become live citations, so their reference appears by itself in *Referencias bibliográficas*; for a work outside the library it proposes its APA reference, added under the list (with italics) and sorted with the rest in the export. A citation typed by hand whose reference you wrote there no longer counts as missing in Requisitos. |
| **Citation support (Respaldo)** | Back every sentence of the thesis with your library without leaving the editor. Put the caret in a sentence (or select the claim) and press **Respaldar** (⌘⌥E, the toolbar, the floating toolbar on a selection, or the card on a flagged sentence). All the papers of a thesis library are about the same topics, so words alone can't tell which one backs a claim: the model first reads the **catalog of your library** (each paper's title and what its reading guide says it studies and finds, in Spanish like the thesis) and picks the papers likeliest to back it, with the literature's English terms; the offline search (BM25 over passages of every page, reference lists left out, highlights and the guide's key findings weighing more) then takes the best passages **inside those papers** plus the best of the whole library, and the model checks up to 24 of them against the sentence, with your model and effort. When none backs it, it **reads the likeliest papers whole**, as the assistant does (*Leer a fondo* runs it on demand). Each card shows the paper's own words (every quote is checked against the paper's text and shown on the page where it really is; if the model paraphrased, the closest real sentence stands in), the Spanish translation, the **page** (the article's printed page; click it to open the PDF there with the passage marked) and a verdict: **lo apoya**, **lo matiza** (e.g. a correlational study for a causal claim, another population), **lo contradice** or *no alcanza*, with one line on why. Sources the sentence already cites, live or typed by hand, show as cited. **Citar** inserts the APA 7 (Spanish) citation before the sentence's final period (*con p. N* adds the article's printed page), and the reference appears by itself in *Referencias bibliográficas*; a second source joins the same parenthesis in alphabetical order, «(Bass, 1985; Popper y Mayseless, 2003)», whether the citation there is live or typed by hand. When the sentence names a course instrument (ECR-RS, EQ-29, HSA-TFL, LSPS, CCO), its references from the framework are listed too, citable in one click when the paper is in your library. **Oraciones sin cita**: the toolbar counts the claims of the body that carry no citation (live or typed by hand, like «Bass (1985)»), leaving out questions, very short sentences, your results (statistics, tables, figures) and your own framing («este trabajo», hypotheses, method); turn on the dashed underline to see them in the text, go through them in the Respaldo tab, search support for each, or mark one **Es mía**. Requisitos counts them as a point to review. Within the UCEMA AI policy (8.e): it finds and checks sources, never writes your text, and the checker is listed in *Aplicado en el asistente*. Without a connected account the offline search still runs and shows the passages, unverified. |
| **Notes** | Rich editor with Markdown shortcuts. Quotes keep a link to their source page, and quotes not already in Spanish are translated automatically: the Spanish version (with the citation) goes first and the original stays right below. Many quotes at once, like *Highlights → To note*, are translated in a single request. **Live citations** (⌘⇧C) re-render when you edit metadata or change citation style. Side-by-side note next to the paper (⌘⇧N). Export to Markdown with a References section. |
| **Assistant** | Always answers in Spanish (quotes stay verbatim, with a translation). Streams answers grounded in the full text, with clickable page citations `[S1, p. 4]`. Quick actions (summarize, relevance to my thesis, critique, key quotes; for notes: organize ideas, find gaps, suggest sources, polish). One click adds an answer to a note with real citations. Knows your thesis title and research question. |
| **Whiteboard** | [Excalidraw](https://excalidraw.com) in its own tab (⌘⌥B, or *Whiteboard* in the sidebar) to sketch ideas. It runs in a persistent, sandboxed session, so your drawing is still there after a restart; ⌘= / ⌘- / ⌘0 zoom the canvas. Use Excalidraw's menu to save a `.excalidraw` file now and then as a backup. **The assistant draws on it:** the general assistant sits next to the board and sees what's drawn, so you can ask for a diagram («dibujá el modelo conceptual de mi tesis», «un flujo con las etapas de mi metodología», «una matriz 2×2 para clasificar mis papers») and talk about what's there. Flowcharts, sequence and class diagrams come from Mermaid, laid out by Excalidraw; concept maps, matrices and timelines from shapes the assistant places itself. Each drawing lands below what's already there, framed in view, as editable Excalidraw shapes; it comes as a checklist like any change, and Deshacer takes it back off the board (Excalidraw's own ⌘Z works too). |
| **Tasks & timeline** | A to-do list for the thesis (⌘⌥D, or *Tasks* in the sidebar), grouped into Vencidas / Hoy / Próximos 7 días / Más adelante / Sin fecha / Hechas. Type a task with the date at the end and it's read for you: «Entregar capítulo 2 viernes», «Reunión con el tutor 15/11», «Revisar en 2 semanas», plus `!` for high priority. Each task has a status (por hacer / en curso / hecha), priority, start and due dates, an optional linked paper and notes; milestones are single key dates. **Cronograma** shows the dated tasks as a Gantt chart by day, week or month, with a line for today: drag a bar to move it, drag its ends to change when it starts or is due. What's late or due today is counted on the sidebar and listed on Home. **The assistant manages the plan too:** next to your tasks it sees the whole list, today's date and your library, so you can ask it to plan the thesis up to the deadline (it can create the course plan itself, and save your 2 topics, hypotheses and demographics to the Marco), ask for **Plan de hoy** (1–3 tasks and a pomodoro to start) or a **Revisión semanal** (focus by stage, words added, what slipped, proposed rescheduling), pick what to do today, reschedule what's late, split the open task into steps, or anything in your own words («pasá lo de esta semana al lunes»). It proposes the changes as a checklist in the chat; untick what you don't want, apply with one click, and undo if needed. Kept in `.data/tasks.json`. |
| **Pomodoro** | Each task can carry a pomodoro plan: focus and break lengths (25/5, 50/10, 90/20 or your own) and how many sessions it takes, with a long break every four. Hit ▶ on a task row (or *Empezar* in its detail) and the timer runs what's left of the plan; it lives in the tab bar while you work anywhere in the app, with pause, +5 min, skip, reset and stop (each undoable). Phase ends play a short cue and, when the window is in the background, a system notification. Rows show the progress («2/4») and the detail shows the time focused. The timer keeps time from an end timestamp, so it survives sleep and restarts. Quick add takes the count too («Leer Porter 2 pomodoros hoy»), and the timer's controls are in ⌘K. **The assistant runs it as well:** it sees each task's plan and the timer's state, so you can ask it to size tasks («dividí esto en pasos de 2 pomodoros»), plan your day in pomodoros, or create a task and start the timer on it in one go («arrancá 2 pomodoros leyendo Porter»), then pause, skip or stop it; as with everything it does, the changes come as a checklist with one Undo. Marking the running task done ends its timer. Ported from [Keel](https://jleonstack.github.io/notch-pomodoro/). Sessions are kept in `.data/sessions.json`. |
| **Knowledge wiki** | Everything you've saved while reading, in one place (⌘⌥K, or *Knowledge* in the sidebar): highlights (Spanish first, original below), your comments on them, notes, a glossary built from every reading guide (A–Z, the same term across papers together) and your recall answers in your own words. Search across all of it (accents and case don't matter, matches are marked), filter highlights by color, or open a paper's page: its TL;DR and relevance, highlights by page, the notes that cite it, its terms and your recall answers. Every item opens where it came from: the exact page and highlight in the PDF, or the note. The assistant next to it answers from what you saved, with clickable page citations: main themes, connections and contradictions across papers, gaps, or a skeleton of the literature review. Nothing extra is stored; it's built from your library. |
| **Citing** | APA 7, Harvard, MLA 9 and Chicago references. **APA 7 details**: page ranges with an en dash and full numbers (615–625), «Artículo e1234» for journals that number articles instead of pages, chapters and conference papers as «En A. Editor (Ed.), *Libro* (pp. 45–67). Editorial.», reports and theses with the title in italics, hyphenated initials (J.-P.), entities and tags from Crossref cleaned («&amp;» → «&»), «p.» / «pp.» in the text. Crossref brings the article number and the editors too, and on launch fills what references lack (only empty fields) for papers with a DOI. *Info → Detalles* flags titles in Title Case (APA uses sentence case for articles) with a «Tipo oración» button, and *Publicación* has the article number and, for chapters, the editors. In-text citations, BibTeX per paper or for the whole library. **Several sources in one parenthesis**: in *Citar* (⌘⇧C) ⌘↩ or the + on a paper adds it to the citation and keeps the list open, then ↩ inserts «(Bass, 1985; Popper y Mayseless, 2003)» in alphabetical order; citing right next to a citation, live or typed by hand, joins that parenthesis. Copies of a paper in the library show once. Per-note bibliography. **Printed page numbers**: citations with a page show the page printed on the article, not the PDF's (PDF page 3 of an article on pp. 615–625 is cited as p. 617), in the thesis, notes, copied quotes and the Word/PDF export, while every jump still opens the right PDF page. The numbering is read from the page numbers in each page's running head or footer, or from the page range when the PDF has none; *Info → Detalles → Numeración de páginas* shows what was found and lets you correct it («la página 3 del PDF es la página impresa 617»). The reader shows the printed page next to the PDF's. |
| **Everywhere** | ⌘K command palette, light/dark/system theme, resizable panes, undo for deletions. |

## Architecture

```
src/main/        Electron main: window, menu, IPC, JSON store, Crossref, Codex and Claude bridges
src/preload/     Typed, sandboxed `window.api` bridge
src/shared/      Domain types + API contract shared by both sides
src/renderer/    React UI (zustand store, pdf.js reader, TipTap editor, assistant)
  components/ui/      shadcn/ui primitives (Radix + Tailwind)
  components/motion/  beUI animated components (tabs, color selector, toasts, …)
  components/agents/  beUI chat components (message scroller, prompt input)
```

- **UI stack:** Tailwind CSS v4 (tokens in `styles/globals.css`, pdf.js/editor rules in `styles/content.css`), [shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com) for dialogs, menus, selects and the ⌘K palette, and [beUI](https://beui.dev) (built on Motion) for spring tabs, color pickers, the toast stack, shimmer loading states and the chat composer. Add more with `bunx shadcn@latest add <name>` or `bunx shadcn@latest add @beui/<name>`.

- **ChatGPT connection:** `src/main/codex.ts` keeps one warm `codex app-server` process and speaks its JSON-RPC over stdio. Each paper or note gets its own thread, and the paper text goes in the thread's developer instructions (so it is prompt-cached). The thread is **pre-warmed** when you open a paper, so your first question doesn't wait on setup. Codex's machine-access tools are disabled and the sandbox is read-only: it is used purely as a chat model.
- **Claude connection:** `src/main/claude.ts` runs one warm `claude -p` process per chat in streaming-JSON mode, with the paper text as the system prompt (written to a temp file, since it can be too large for argv). Every tool is off, no settings, MCP servers, skills or CLAUDE.md are loaded, nothing is persisted, and API-key variables are stripped so it always bills to the subscription. Which bridge answers is decided by the model id (`src/shared/ai.ts`).
- **Speed:** only pages near the viewport render. Zoom renders off-screen and then swaps, so there's no blank flash. The last 4 tabs stay mounted, so switching is instant. Streaming markdown is re-parsed at most once per frame. Writes are optimistic in the UI and persisted with debounced, atomic writes in main.
- **Security:** `contextIsolation`, `sandbox`, a strict CSP, no remote content, and external links open in your browser. The one exception is the whiteboard: a `<webview>` that may only load `excalidraw.com`, with no preload, no Node, its own `persist:excalidraw` session and only clipboard/file permissions (see `guardWhiteboard` in `src/main/index.ts`).

## Laws of UX, applied

Every law on [lawsofux.com](https://lawsofux.com), and where it lives in the app.

| Law | Where |
|---|---|
| **Aesthetic-Usability Effect** | Conduit brand: warm neutrals, Neue Montreal + Season Mix, flower art with halftone grain, soft depth, light and dark themes. |
| **Choice Overload** | Library filters and search, library grouped by status, Cite panel folds rarely used fields, at most 3 follow-up suggestions. |
| **Chunking** | Library grouped by Reading / To read / Read, notes by Pinned, highlights by page, Cite split into Citation / Details / Publication / Tags & abstract. |
| **Cognitive Bias** | Every claim carries a clickable page chip and the composer says "Click a cited page to check it". Critique is a one-click action. AI-filled metadata asks you to double-check. |
| **Cognitive Load** | Everything is collapsible and remembers its state. Collapsed panels become rails. The assistant's context card is one line until you open it. |
| **Doherty Threshold (<400 ms)** | Warm AI threads, token streaming, instant "Reading and thinking…" with a seconds counter after 3 s, copy turns into a check, optimistic saves, instant tab switching. |
| **Fitts's Law** | Rails on the window edges with 36 px targets, full-width section headers, full-width suggestion cards, selection toolbar right above the selected text. |
| **Flow** | Follow-up suggestions under each answer, "Jump to latest" while scrolled up, single-key shortcuts on selections, no modal interruptions. |
| **Goal-Gradient Effect** | Setup checklist with a progress bar, library "n/m read" bar, per-paper reading progress, a toast when you finish a paper. |
| **Hick's Law** | Four quick actions, one marked "Start here", each with a one-line hint. Depth is Fast / Balanced / Thorough, not raw parameters. |
| **Jakob's Law** | Three-pane layout, tabs, ⌘K palette, Finder-style disclosure chevrons, ⌥-click to fold every section in a group, VS Code-style rails, standard macOS menus. |
| **Law of Common Region** | Context card, setup card, recent lists and the Citation block each sit on their own surface. |
| **Law of Prägnanz** | One chevron, one pill marker and one chip shape, reused everywhere. |
| **Law of Proximity** | Message actions sit under their message, section actions sit in the section header, quotes sit above the question they belong to. |
| **Law of Similarity** | One collapsible component for every section. Papers are orange and notes green everywhere. Citation chips look the same in chat and notes. Links are blue, text is not. |
| **Law of Uniform Connectedness** | Highlight color bars tie a quote to its color, marker pills tie a section to its category, the quote rule ties a passage to its question. |
| **Mental Model** | The context card says exactly what the assistant reads (paper, sources, thesis, model). Collapsing a panel leaves a rail, so nothing "disappears". |
| **Miller's Law** | Lists are chunked into small groups. Palette shows ≤ 8 results per group. At most 3 follow-ups. |
| **Occam's Razor** | Panel toggles live on the panels (no duplicates in the tab bar). Panels only show what the active document needs. |
| **Paradox of the Active User** | Guidance in context: "select a passage and press A", shortcuts in every tooltip, ⌥-click hint on section headers. |
| **Pareto Principle** | Citation and Details open by default; Publication and Tags & abstract fold. The assistant gets the most attention. |
| **Parkinson's Law** | Metadata fills itself (DOI → Crossref → AI), one-click follow-ups, one-click "Add to note", commit-on-blur fields. |
| **Peak-End Rule** | Answers end with "Add to note" and next steps. Finishing a paper is celebrated. Errors end with "Try again". Clearing a chat, removing a highlight or deleting a note offers Undo. |
| **Postel's Law** | PDFs dropped anywhere, DOIs with or without `https://doi.org/`, authors as "Family, Given" or "Given Family", tags with or without `#`, citations like `[S1, pp. 11, 15–16]`. |
| **Selective Attention** | A dot on the Assistant tab or rail when an answer is being written or arrives out of view. Nothing is styled like an ad or banner. |
| **Serial Position Effect** | Rail: search first, settings last. Panel collapse at the far right. Message actions: "Add to note" first, Regenerate last. |
| **Tesler's Law** | The app builds the assistant's context, formats citations and bibliographies, and keeps citations live. |
| **Von Restorff Effect** | Blue is reserved for AI; one recommended action. Status uses dots **and** labels (never color alone), and motion respects Reduce Motion. |
| **Working Memory** | Collapsed sections keep a count and summary. Follow-ups skip what you already asked. The quote you're asking about stays pinned above the composer. |
| **Zeigarnik Effect** | Rails keep counts and badges, the setup card says what's next even when folded, "Answer continues below" while streaming. |
